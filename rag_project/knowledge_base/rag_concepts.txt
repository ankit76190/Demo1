RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation, a technique that combines information retrieval with text generation.
RAG was introduced by Lewis et al. in the 2020 paper "Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks".
The key idea of RAG is to retrieve relevant documents and provide them as context to a language model before generating an answer.
RAG helps language models answer questions about topics not seen during training or that require up-to-date information.
The retrieval component of RAG searches a knowledge base to find documents most relevant to the user's query.
The generation component of RAG uses a language model to produce a coherent answer conditioned on the retrieved documents.
Embeddings are dense vector representations of text that capture semantic meaning; similar texts have similar embeddings.
Cosine similarity measures the angle between two embedding vectors and is commonly used to rank document relevance.
Chunking is the process of splitting long documents into smaller overlapping pieces before indexing them.
A vector store (also called a vector database) stores embeddings and supports efficient similarity search.
Popular vector stores include FAISS, Chroma, Pinecone, Weaviate, and Qdrant.
The context window of a language model limits how much retrieved text can be passed alongside a query.
Hybrid search combines dense vector retrieval with traditional keyword (BM25) retrieval for better results.
Re-ranking models score candidate documents a second time to improve the quality of the final retrieved set.
RAG reduces hallucinations by grounding the language model's response in factual retrieved documents.
Evaluation metrics for RAG include faithfulness (is the answer supported by the context?) and answer relevance.
LangChain and LlamaIndex are popular frameworks that simplify building RAG pipelines in Python.
